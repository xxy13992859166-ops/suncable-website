Solar Cable Storage & Handling: Best Practices Guide

📅 October 28, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read 🏭 By JunlinChicheng Team

Most cable that fails early was damaged before it was energised. A drum left in the sun for a season, a run pulled over a sharp edge, a cut end left open in the rain — none of these show up at commissioning, and all of them shorten the service life of a product that is supposed to last twenty-five years. The rules below cost nothing to follow and are the cheapest reliability measure on any project.

Drums: upright, off the ground, restrained

  • Store drums upright on their flanges, never lying on the side of the barrel.
  • Keep them off bare ground on dunnage so moisture does not wick into the outer layers.
  • Chock them so they cannot roll, and never drop a drum off a tailgate — impact damage to the inner layers is invisible from outside.
  • Roll a drum only to move it a short distance; for anything longer, lift it.

UV, heat and cold before installation

XLPO is designed for decades of sunlight, but that assumes installation. Cable stored outdoors for months on an uncovered drum sees the same UV with no thermal path to shed heat, and the outer wraps take the worst of it. Keep drums covered or indoors, and if a drum has been outside for a full season, inspect the outer layer before you pull it into a run.

Cold matters too. Below freezing, insulation is stiffer and less tolerant of sharp bends. If cable has been stored at low temperature, let it come back toward ambient before pulling, or handle it with a larger bend radius.

Bend radius and pulling tension

Two numbers prevent most mechanical damage:

SituationRule of thumb
Fixed, installed bendAbout 4× the overall cable diameter
During pullingAbout 15× the overall diameter, never a kink
Pulling tensionPull the conductor, not the jacket; use a swivel and never yank a stalled run

A kink is permanent. Even if the cable looks straight afterwards, the conductor and insulation at that point have been deformed, and it will be the first place to fail under thermal cycling.

Seal every cut end

An open end is a wick. Moisture travels along the strands by capillary action and shows up much later as corrosion at a termination metres away. Tape or heat-shrink every cut end, including offcuts returned to store, and never leave a partially used drum with the end hanging in a puddle.

On an active site

Once installation starts, the risks change: machinery traffic, sharp tray edges, hot work, and cable left lying where the next trade will walk on it. Route runs off the floor as soon as they are pulled, protect edges at every transition, and keep connectors in their packaging until the moment they are mated.

Inspect before you pull, not after. A two-minute walk along a drum before installation catches jacket damage while it is still the supplier's problem. After the cable is in the tray, it is yours.
