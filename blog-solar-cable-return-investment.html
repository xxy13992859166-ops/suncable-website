Solar Cable ROI: How Quality Cables Save Money Over 25 Years

📅 October 28, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read 🏭 By JunlinChicheng Team

Cable is a small line in a PV bill of materials and a large line in the lifetime economics of the plant. The reason is simple: everything else in the array can be reached, unbolted and replaced in an afternoon. Cable is installed first, buried under the array, and stays there for twenty-five years. That asymmetry is the whole investment case.

Cost per metre is the wrong denominator

The useful number is cost per metre-year of service, not cost per metre. Two cables at different prices stop being comparable the moment one of them needs to be replaced, or loses output, before the plant's design life is over. Written as a comparison rather than a formula:

  • Certified cable: higher unit cost, no planned replacement, output held for the design life.
  • Uncertified cable: lower unit cost, plus a replacement event somewhere in year 5–12, plus the labour to reach and re-pull the run, plus the generation lost while it is down.

The replacement event is what dominates. Re-pulling a string run on a commercial roof means scaffolding or rope access, isolating the string, and a second commissioning — costs that dwarf the saved unit price of the original drum.

Derating and voltage drop are permanent losses

A cable with a lower temperature rating, or a smaller cross-section chosen to hit a price, does not fail — it just runs hotter and drops more volts, every day, for the life of the plant. Those losses are silent: no alarm, no fault code, just a plant that produces slightly less than the model predicted. On a long DC run, one step down in cross-section is often worth more than a percent of annual yield.

Warranty is only worth the entity behind it

"25-year warranty" appears on almost every PV cable datasheet. The question worth asking is who performs under it: which legal entity, in which country, and what evidence they require. A warranty from a trading company that re-labels drums is a promise with no factory behind it. Ask for the certificate holder's name and check it matches the company issuing the warranty.

A one-question test for any supplier: "If a string fails in year eight because of your cable, who pays for the access and re-pull?" A supplier with a real factory and a real certificate answers with a process. A reseller changes the subject.
