The six questions we were asked most in 2026

This is not a market report with numbers we cannot substantiate. It is a summary of what buyers actually asked us across the year, and the short answer to each — the questions repeat because the same three or four decisions decide most purchases.

1. "Is this certificate real?"

Take the number and check it on the issuing body's own database — Certipedia for TÜV Rheinland, Product iQ for UL. Then read the scope line: a certificate for 4 mm² does not cover 6 mm². Most doubts end there.

2. "Can you meet this price?"

Usually the comparison is not like for like. FOB against CIF, a different size mix, a different drum length, or a certificate scope that is narrower than the one specified. Compare per size, per drum, delivered to the same port.

3. "What is your MOQ?"

1 km per colour on standard sizes. Below that the setup cost dominates, and a supplier who accepts it is either losing money or substituting material.

4. "How fast can you ship?"

7–15 days for standard specifications. What moves it: non-standard colours, private label printing, and third-party inspection. Ask which of those apply rather than asking for an earlier date.

5. "Why is 120 °C in your datasheet if the standard says 90 °C?"

Because they are two different numbers. −40 °C to +90 °C is the ambient operating range; 120 °C is the maximum conductor temperature, qualified by long-duration ageing testing. Any datasheet that presents 120 °C as an ambient operating range is misreading its own certification.

6. "Who honours the warranty?"

The entity that issues it, in the country where the claim is made. A 25-year claim from a company that re-labels drums is a promise without a factory. Ask for the certificate holder's name and check it matches.

What stays constant

Across every market we shipped to this year, the purchases that went well had the same shape: a stated specification, a certificate that matched it, a sample before the bulk order, and a drum plan agreed before production. None of that is new, which is why it keeps working.
