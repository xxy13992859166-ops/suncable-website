Residential is where oversizing is cheapest

A home array is small, the run from roof to inverter is short, and the cable is a trivial share of the installed cost. That combination makes it the one place where choosing a size up, and choosing a certified cable, costs almost nothing — and where the cost of getting it wrong lands on a homeowner rather than a maintenance budget.

Sizing in three steps

  1. String current. Take the module's short-circuit current and apply the relevant multiplier from the installation standard for the local condition.
  2. Voltage drop. Use the loop length — up the roof, across, and back down. Keep the DC drop inside the 1–2 % design target; on a 20 m loop this is rarely the constraint, on a detached garage it often is.
  3. Ambient correction. Cable in a loft or behind a roof tile runs hotter than the outside air temperature on the datasheet.

Battery additions and the second run

If a battery is likely within a few years, leave capacity in the route now: a spare duct from roof to plant room costs a fraction of re-opening a finished wall. The battery itself needs a different cable — low-smoke halogen-free DC cable sized on current, not on voltage drop — so plan it as a second circuit rather than re-using the PV run.

Beware the kit cable

Many small systems ship with pre-terminated cable of unknown origin. It is often the weakest part of an otherwise sound install. If you cannot identify the standard the cable was made to, replace it — the cost is small and the alternative is discovering the problem in year six.
