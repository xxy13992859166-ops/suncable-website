Solar Cable Fault Diagnosis: 7 Common Problems and Solutions

📅 October 28, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read 🏭 By JunlinChicheng Team

PV strings fail slowly and then all at once. The useful signal is almost never a sudden open circuit — it is a string running a few percent below its neighbours for months, a connector warmer than the one next to it, or a jacket that has stopped being flexible. These are the seven faults we see most often on installed arrays, how to recognise each one, and what actually prevents it.

1. Hot connector or crimp

Symptom: a connector that is noticeably warm, discoloured, or shows a rising resistance reading between visits. Cause: incomplete crimp, wrong barrel for the cable size, or a joint that loosened under thermal cycling. Fix: cut back and re-terminate with the specified tool; check a handful of joints per string, not just the failed one.

2. Water ingress at the connector

Symptom: green or black corrosion on the contact, a string that fails after rain, sealant squeezed out of the gland. Cause: O-ring not seated, nut not torqued, cable outside the gland's diameter range, or two different brands mated together. Fix: replace the pair, match gland to cable OD, and keep one brand per string.

3. UV cracking and jacket crazing

Symptom: surface crazing, chalking, or a jacket that cracks when bent at a bend radius that should be comfortable. Cause: PVC or under-cross-linked compound sold as solar cable; or cable left on a drum in the open for a season before installation. Fix: specify XLPO cable tested to IEC 62930 or EN 50618, and cover drums on site.

4. Rodent damage

Symptom: chewed jacket, usually at trays, wall penetrations or the first metre above ground. Cause: exposed routing in agricultural or rural sites. Fix: route in conduit at vulnerable sections, backfill trenches properly, and prefer a harder jacket in these runs rather than relying on the standard cable alone.

5. Mechanical damage at supports and edges

Symptom: a clean nick or flattened section where the cable crosses a tray lip, clamp or roof edge. Cause: sharp edges, over-tight clamps, or cable pulled around a corner below its bend radius. Fix: add edge protection, use cable ties at the correct tension, and respect the radius rule — as a rule of thumb, four times the overall diameter for a fixed bend.

6. Thermal cycling loosening terminations

Symptom: torque marks that no longer line up after a year; intermittent string readings. Cause: dissimilar expansion rates and joints that were never re-torqued after commissioning. Fix: include a re-torque check in the first annual service.

7. Mismatched cross-section and long runs

Symptom: a string that is consistently low on output with no fault found. Cause: undersized cable on a long DC run, so voltage drop eats the difference. Fix: recalculate against the actual run length; a typical design target is to keep DC drop to 1–2 %.

Diagnose by comparison. Measure every string at the same irradiance and compare them with each other. A fault shows up as one string that differs from its neighbours; chasing one string in isolation usually finds nothing.
