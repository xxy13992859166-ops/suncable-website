Solar Cable for Southern Africa: Heat, UV and Compliance

Real-world case study of a large-scale solar installation in South Africa. Cable selection, logistics, and commissioning results.

What southern African sites demand from cable

We are not going to publish a project case study with figures we cannot substantiate. What we can describe honestly is the specification pattern that southern African projects keep arriving at, because the environment drives it: high irradiance, high ambient temperature, dust, and — in coastal installations — salt.

Heat is a derating problem before it is a cable problem

An ambient of 40 °C at the site is not the temperature in a roof void or inside a combiner enclosure, which can run 15–20 K higher. Two consequences follow: the current-carrying capacity used in the design must be derated for the real enclosure temperature, and the cable's temperature class has to be read correctly — −40 °C to +90 °C ambient operation, with 120 °C as the maximum conductor temperature, not a working ambient.

UV, dust and cleaning cycles

  • UV: XLPO insulation to IEC 62930 or EN 50618 is the baseline; PVC-jacketed "solar" cable does not survive the exposure.
  • Dust: accumulates on trays and in connectors; plan cleaning access and do not rely on rain to clear a horizontal run.
  • Cleaning: water and brushes at pressure are a mechanical load on cable and connectors — specify glands and supports that tolerate it.

Coastal and industrial salt

Within a few kilometres of the coast, the failure mode shifts to corrosion at terminations. The mitigation is not a different cable so much as better joints: IP68 connectors seated on the correct gland, contacts inspected at the first annual service, and no bare copper left exposed. In industrial and mining environments the same applies with sulphur and dust added.

Compliance paperwork in the region

South African projects typically ask for SABS-related evidence; Nigerian projects for SONCAP; Gulf projects for SASO or equivalent. Those are market-access requirements layered on top of the product certificate, not substitutes for it. Our export markets page sets out which certificate applies where.
