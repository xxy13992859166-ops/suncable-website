10 Questions to Ask a Solar Cable Manufacturer in China

📅 October 28, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read 🏭 By JunlinChicheng Team

The title of this page used to promise a list of ten manufacturers. There is no honest way to publish such a list: Chinese PV cable capacity changes every quarter, most "top ten" lists are paid placements, and a name on someone else's list tells you nothing about whether that factory can issue the certificate your customer's inspector will ask for. So this page answers the question you actually have — how do I tell a real PV cable factory from a trading company that will subcontract my order — with ten questions you can ask any supplier today.

Why the questions matter more than the list

PV cable is a commodity in appearance and not in fact. Two drums can look identical and differ in conductor stranding class, insulation compound, wall thickness tolerance and weather-resistance classification. The difference does not show up in a photo or a price table; it shows up in year eight on a rooftop, in a failed inspection, or in a string that quietly loses output. A supplier who can answer the ten questions below with documents is a different kind of company from one who answers with adjectives.

The ten questions

  1. Which standard does the cable hold, and who tested it? For IEC 62930 or EN 50618 the certificate should come from a recognised body (TÜV Rheinland, TÜV SÜD, DEKRA, UL) and carry a certificate number you can look up yourself.
  2. Does the certificate cover the size I am buying? A certificate for 4 mm² does not automatically cover 10 mm². Ask for the certificate scope, not a logo.
  3. What conductor class is used? PV cable is normally Class 5 flexible tinned copper to IEC 60228. Aluminium or copper-clad conductors are sold as equivalents in some markets and are not equivalent.
  4. What is the insulation compound? Cross-linked polyolefin (XLPO) is the standard for outdoor PV. PVC appears in cheap "solar" cable and does not carry the same UV or thermal performance.
  5. What is the rated temperature window? A correct answer distinguishes ambient operating range (−40 °C to +90 °C for EN 50618 cable) from maximum conductor temperature (120 °C).
  6. Can I see a type-test report, not just a certificate? Thermal endurance, cold bend, ozone, water immersion and voltage withstand data should exist as a document.
  7. Are you the factory or a trading company? Ask for the production address and the extrusion capacity. Then check that the address matches the certificate holder.
  8. What is your MOQ and your real lead time? A factory that states a clean MOQ (for example 1 km per colour) and a 7–15 day standard lead time is easier to plan around than one that says "depends".
  9. Will you ship a sample before the bulk order? Free or paid, a sample you can cut, measure and bend tells you more than a datasheet.
  10. Who honours the warranty? A 25-year claim is only worth the entity behind it. Ask who replaces the cable and in which country the claim is handled.

A five-minute check: take the certificate number from the supplier and search it on the issuing body's own database — Certipedia for TÜV Rheinland, Product iQ for UL. If the number does not return the same product description and size range, stop there.
