Solar Cable for Agricultural Solar Projects: Agri-PV Solutions

📅 October 28, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read 🏭 By JunlinChicheng Team

Agricultural PV is the least forgiving environment a cable will ever see. A rooftop array on an office building deals with sun and heat; an array over a field or on a barn roof deals with irrigation spray, condensation cycles, ammonia, rodents, dust, machinery and, increasingly, people walking under it with tools. Selection decisions that are optional elsewhere are not optional here.

Long runs make voltage drop the first decision

Agri-PV layouts spread out: arrays follow field boundaries, inverters sit at the edge of the plot, and DC runs of a few hundred metres are normal. At that distance the cable size is set by voltage drop, not by current-carrying capacity. Work the calculation on the real route length — including vertical drops and the loop through the tracker — before you fix a size, and keep the DC loss inside the 1–2 % design target. Oversizing one step is usually cheaper than accepting a permanent 3 % loss for twenty-five years.

Irrigation, condensation and standing water

Sprinkler overspray and overnight condensation mean the cable sees water far more often than the "outdoor" label implies. Two practical rules:

  • Terminate connectors above the local water line and never at the low point of a run.
  • Use IP68 connectors and seat them properly — a gland that is a size too large will pass water under spray even though the part is rated IP68.

Where cable is buried between rows, use a duct or a rated direct-burial construction, keep the trench clear of field drainage, and record the route so the next subsoiling pass does not find it.

Livestock buildings: ammonia and rodents

Inside poultry or cattle housing the aggressive agents are ammonia and rodents, not sunlight. Route cable in conduit, keep it off ledges where dust collects, and avoid PVC-jacketed product in these buildings — the point of a halogen-free XLPO construction is that it does not embrittle the way PVC does under chemical and thermal load. For runs to fans, feeders and lighting, a steel or rigid conduit is often the cheapest long-term answer.

Dust, heat and elevated mounting

Panels over crops are usually mounted higher than a standard rooftop, so more cable hangs in air and moves with wind. Support it at shorter intervals than you would on a roof, keep connectors off the ground, and derate for the higher ambient temperature under the array rather than for the weather-station reading.

Plan the cable route with the agronomist, not after. Headland turning circles, irrigation mains and subsoiling depth all constrain where a cable can run. Moving a route on paper costs nothing; moving it after commissioning costs a season.

Specifying the cable

For agri-PV we normally quote H1Z2Z2-K single core (1 kV, 1.5–6 mm²) for string runs and PV1-F 2-core (0.6/1 kV, 1.0–6.0 mm²) where the red/black pair simplifies pulling. Both are XLPO insulated, rated −40 °C to +90 °C ambient with 120 °C maximum conductor temperature, and available with TÜV, UL and CE documentation.
