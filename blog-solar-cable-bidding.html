Solar Cable Bidding Guide: Winning International Tenders

How to prepare competitive solar cable bids. Documentation requirements, pricing strategies, and qualification standards.

💬 Chat on WhatsApp

Tenders are lost on documents, not on price

On an international tender the cable package is small and the paperwork is not. Most disqualifications we see are technical: a certificate that does not cover the size tendered, a test report in the wrong language, a datasheet that contradicts the certificate, or a bid that responds to a different standard than the one specified. All of them are avoidable before submission.

The document set that survives review

  • Certificate with scope by size. Not the cover page — the schedule showing which cross-sections are covered.
  • Type-test report for thermal endurance, cold bend and UV, with the laboratory named.
  • Batch test certificate for the production run, if the tender asks for pre-delivery evidence.
  • Datasheet matching the certificate — same standard, same temperature class, same conductor class.
  • Declaration of conformity and, where required, the material declarations for the insulation.

Read the technical specification before pricing

Clause to checkWhy bids fail on it
Standard referenceQuoting IEC 62930 where EN 50618 is specified (or the reverse)
Temperature classOffering −40/+90 when the spec asks for a stated maximum conductor temperature
Conductor classClass 5 required, Class 2 offered
Test evidenceType test offered where batch test is required
Delivery termsCIF priced where the schedule is FOB port of loading

Price the scope, not the cable

A tender is usually won or lost on the landed cost of the package. Build the price from the same four blocks every time: cable by size, drums and packing, freight and insurance to the stated Incoterm, and the documentation and testing the tender demands. Two bids that differ by a few percent per metre are usually differing on drum returns or on who pays for the pre-shipment inspection.

Lead time is a bid qualification

State the lead time you can hold, and what could change it. A 7–15 day standard lead time with a clear statement of what pushes it out is more persuasive to an evaluator than an optimistic date that slips after award — the second one turns into a claim.

What we provide for tenders

Certificate scope by size, type-test references, batch certificates and datasheets for PV1-F, H1Z2Z2-K, MC4 connectors, storage and AC cable, in English. Send the tender's cable schedule and the technical clauses to our quotation desk and we will come back with a compliant offer or an explicit list of deviations.
