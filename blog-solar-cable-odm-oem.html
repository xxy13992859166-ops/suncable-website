What private label actually changes

ODM and OEM mean different things in this trade and are often used interchangeably. OEM is your specification, your marking, our production. ODM is our design and specification, your brand. The distinction decides who owns the certificate, who carries the liability if the cable fails, and whether the marking on the drum can legally appear at all.

The certificate question comes first

A certificate belongs to a named holder for a defined product. Putting your brand on a drum does not transfer it. Three arrangements exist, and they are not equivalent:

  • Relabelling. The cable is certified under the factory's name; your brand appears on the drum and the delivery documents. Cheapest, but the certificate never names you.
  • Certificate extension. Your brand is added to the existing certificate as a licensed mark, with the certificate body's agreement. Requires paperwork and time.
  • Own certification. The product is certified under your name. Slowest and most expensive, but the only version that survives a tender asking whose certificate it is.

MOQ and the economics of a private label run

Private label adds setup cost: artwork, print cylinder or ink jet programming, drum labels, and a separate batch record. That cost is spread over the run, so the realistic question is not "what is the MOQ" but "what run makes the setup negligible". Our standard MOQ is 1 km per colour; a private label run needs more than that to be worth doing for both sides, and we will say so rather than take an order that loses money on both sides.
