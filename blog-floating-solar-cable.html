Why floating PV is a different cable problem

On a floating array the cable spends part of its life in water and all of its life in motion. Pontoons rise and fall with level and wave, the array rotates slowly around its mooring, and the cable between floats is loaded every time the water moves. A cable that survives a rooftop for 25 years can fail in three seasons here if the mechanical design is wrong.

The cable is not the first decision — the route is

Decide where the cable is dry, where it is submerged, and where it is merely splashed, because each zone needs different hardware:

  • On the float — fixed to the pontoon frame, UV-exposed, splashed. Standard XLPO PV cable is appropriate, supported so it does not chafe on the frame edge.
  • Between floats — the moving section. This needs a service loop and a strain-relief, or the movement is transferred into the conductor.
  • Submerged run to shore — the length that must tolerate continuous immersion. Specify the construction and the depth with the supplier in writing; not every PV cable is offered for permanent immersion.

Water ingress happens at the joint, not in the cable

Immersion does not usually damage a sound cable; it finds the terminations. Three rules that prevent most floating-PV moisture failures:

RuleWhy
Terminate above the waterlineJoints are the weak point; keep them dry and reachable
Gland matched to cable ODAn IP68 connector with an oversized gland still leaks
Strain relief both sides of a jointMovement must not arrive at the contact

UV where the cable leaves the water

The shore transition is the harshest metre on the site: wet, sunlit, walked on, and often where the cable bends. Protect it in conduit, keep the bend radius generous, and record the route so a later dredging or anchoring operation does not cut it.

Inspection that actually finds problems

Insulation resistance to earth, measured per string at commissioning and again after the first wet season, is the cheapest early warning available. A string whose reading drifts downward is where water is getting in.
