PV Cable vs Electrical Cable: What's the Difference?

Can you use regular electrical cable for solar panels? Learn the key differences between PV cable and standard electrical cable. Why it matters for safety and warranty.

Can You Use Regular Electrical Cable for Solar?

The short answer is NO - and here's why it matters. Regular electrical cable (like THHN, PVC insulated wire) is designed for indoor, protected environments. Solar PV cables are engineered for harsh outdoor conditions: intense UV radiation, temperatures from -40°C to +120°C, constant flexing, and 25+ year outdoor exposure. Using regular cable for solar applications is a fire hazard and will void your panel warranty.

Key Difference #1: UV Resistance

PV cables use XLPO (cross-linked polyolefin) or duromer jackets specifically formulated to resist UV degradation. Regular PVC cables become brittle and crack within 1-2 years of UV exposure. A TUV test requires 5,000+ hours of UV exposure without cracking. Regular electrical cable fails this test in under 500 hours. Once the jacket cracks, moisture enters, causing short circuits and fire risk.

Key Difference #2: Temperature Rating

PV cables are rated for -40°C to +120°C continuous operation, with short-circuit tolerance up to 250°C. Regular electrical cable is typically rated -20°C to +70°C. On a sunny day, solar cable temperatures can reach 85-90°C on dark rooftops. Regular cable insulation softens and degrades at these temperatures, leading to insulation failure and potential fire.

Key Difference #3: Double Insulation

PV cables have dual-layer insulation (inner XLPO + outer duromer jacket) providing extra protection against moisture, chemicals, and physical damage. Regular electrical cable has single-layer PVC insulation. The dual-layer design is what gives PV cables their 25-year design life versus 10-15 years for standard cable.

Key Difference #4: Flexibility

PV cables are designed to be flexible even at low temperatures. They can be bent, routed through clips, and installed in tight spaces without damaging the conductors. Regular cable becomes rigid in cold weather and may crack when bent. For rooftop installations requiring cable routing around obstacles, PV cable flexibility is essential.

Warranty and Certification Implications

All major solar panel manufacturers (Jinko, Longi, Trina, Canadian Solar, JA Solar) require TUV or UL certified PV cables for warranty validation. Using non-certified cable voids the panel warranty. Insurance companies also demand certified PV cables. Regulatory inspections in most countries require TUV/UL certification documentation. The cost of a false economy: $0.30/m saved on cable vs $50,000+ in claimed losses from a fire.

When Regular Cable IS Acceptable

Regular electrical cable CAN be used for: 1) AC side wiring (inverter to grid/panel board) - use THHN/THWN rated for the applicable voltage. 2) Indoor DC wiring in protected conduits. 3) Battery bank connections (use proper battery cable). But for ALL outdoor PV array interconnections (panel to panel, string to combiner), ONLY use TUV/UL certified PV cable.
