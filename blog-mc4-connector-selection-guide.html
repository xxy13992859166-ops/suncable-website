MC4 Connector Selection Guide: Complete Buyer's Guide 2026

📅 October 28, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read 🏭 By JunlinChicheng Team

Most PV array failures that get blamed on cable are actually connector failures. A connector concentrates the current of a whole string into a few square millimetres of contact, sits in the sun for twenty-five years, and is assembled on site by whoever is available. Choosing the right part and crimping it properly matters more than the price difference between two brands.

1. Match the voltage and current rating to the string

Modern strings are designed for 1500 V DC systems, and the connector must carry the string current with margin. Our MC4-Evo2 set is rated 1500 V DC and 1000 V DC, 41 A (32 A for the Evo2-PA4 variant), which covers residential and commercial string sizes. If your string current is close to the rating, derate for temperature: contact resistance rises with heat, and a connector running at its limit in a hot roof void is a hotspot waiting to happen.

2. Check the cable range and the crimp

The connector must match the cable cross-section, not approximately but exactly. Our set accepts 1.5–6 mm² Class 5 tinned copper. Three things decide whether the joint survives:

  • The right crimping tool. A generic plier deforms the barrel unevenly; use the crimper specified for the connector series.
  • Strand integrity. No cut strands, no strands folded back outside the barrel.
  • A pull check. After crimping, the joint should not move under a firm axial pull. If it does, cut it off and redo it — a loose crimp is a future fire, not a future repair.

3. Sealing is a system, not a gasket

IP68 to IEC 60529 is only achieved when the gland compresses against the cable's actual outside diameter and the O-ring seats cleanly. A connector rated IP68 will leak if it is closed on a cable that is too thin, if the O-ring is dry, or if the nut is hand-tightened without the final quarter turn. Torque it, and use the sealing grease supplied with the part, not an arbitrary substitute.

4. Never mate two different brands

"MC4 compatible" means the shape fits, not that the contact system is tested together. Mating brand A to brand B voids both manufacturers' approvals and is one of the most common causes of water ingress and thermal failure on site. Keep one brand per string, and carry spare pairs of the same type.

Thermal cycling is the real test. A joint that measures fine at commissioning can open up after a hundred hot days. Sample-check resistance on a few joints per string after the first summer, and compare strings against each other — a string running a few percent low usually has one bad connector, not a bad module.
