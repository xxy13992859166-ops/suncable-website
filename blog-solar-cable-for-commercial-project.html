Solar Cable for Commercial Projects: Complete Sizing & Procurement Guide

📅 October 28, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read 🏭 By JunlinChicheng Team

A commercial rooftop job is won or lost in procurement, not on the roof. The cable schedule arrives late, the sizes get rounded up "to be safe", the certification does not match what the consultant specified, and the drum lengths do not divide into the runs. Each of those costs money, and only one of them is visible on the invoice.

Start from the cable schedule, not from a price list

Before asking anyone for a price, fix four things: the DC string layout, the run lengths from array to inverter, the AC route to the point of connection, and the ambient temperature the cable will actually sit in. Those four numbers determine every size on the sheet. A supplier quoting against "about 2,000 metres of 4 mm²" is guessing, and so is the price.

Sizing: voltage drop first, then ampacity

On a commercial roof the runs are long enough that voltage drop usually governs. Set the DC target at 1–2 % and check the AC side separately. Then confirm the conductor still carries the current after derating for the temperature in the tray — a cable sized only against the datasheet ampacity, with no derating for a hot roof void, is a common commissioning finding.

Fire, tray and routing

Where cable enters the building it stops being an outdoor product. Check the local requirement for flame propagation and smoke on internal routes, keep DC and AC in separate containment where the standard requires it, and do not run PV cable in the same tray as mains without the segregation the inspector will ask for. On a roof, support intervals and edge protection matter more than the cable choice — most mechanical damage happens at a tray lip, not mid-span.

Documentation for handover

A commercial client will ask for the certificate scope, the batch test certificate, and confirmation that the sizes installed are the sizes certified. Collect them at order time, not at handover: getting a certificate amended after the drum is on site is the slowest possible way to close a project.

Order lengths that divide into runs. Drum lengths that leave a 40 m offcut per run turn into scrap. Send us the run list and we will match drum lengths to it — it usually saves more than a small unit-price difference.
